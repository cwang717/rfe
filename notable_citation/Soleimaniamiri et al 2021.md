The FHWA CARMA Proof-of-Concept report by Soleimaniamiri et al. provides objective evidence that my contribution was used beyond ordinary citation. The report did not merely list my paper as background literature; it relied on the trajectory-optimization framework that I helped create—namely, the use of optimized CAV trajectories to balance fuel/energy performance, driving comfort, traffic delay, and safety/vehicle-dynamics constraints—as part of the technical basis for a federal cooperative driving automation use case. In my prior work on trajectory optimization for connected automated traffic streams, I helped clarify how exact optimization and fast heuristic approaches could be evaluated for practical CAV trajectory smoothing. Soleimaniamiri et al. extended this contribution from a connected automated traffic-stream problem into a USDOT/FHWA CARMA transportation-systems-management application: Dynamic Lane Assignment at signalized corridors. Their proposed CARMA framework incorporated trajectory smoothing for C–ADS-equipped vehicles together with signal and lane optimization, using real-time vehicle information to improve throughput and energy performance while maintaining safety. This shows that my contribution was not simply cited as a scholarly article, but was adopted as a technical foundation for a federal cooperative-automation control architecture that connected vehicle-level trajectory planning with infrastructure-level traffic management.
