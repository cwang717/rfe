**Zhou et al. (2024) provides objective evidence that my contribution was used as a technical foundation for subsequent research on learning-based CAV longitudinal control, rather than being cited only as a publication.** In that work, the authors addressed a deployment-oriented question directly connected to my Contribution 2: how learning-based car-following and longitudinal control methods for CAVs can generalize across different traffic conditions, especially under stop-and-go traffic. Their study did not merely mention my papers in passing. It placed my proactive longitudinal-control work for mixed-flow traffic within the representative body of DRL-based learning car-following control methods, and then extended that line of work by analyzing a deeper theoretical issue: what kinds of training trajectories are needed for behavior cloning and deep reinforcement learning controllers to remain reliable when traffic conditions change. Specifically, Zhou et al. used the same technical setting that my work helped establish—CAV longitudinal control in mixed traffic where human-driven vehicles create oscillations and disturbances—and advanced it into a new framework connecting stop-and-go traffic, persistent excitation, system identification, DNN robustness, and DRL generalizability. This shows that my contribution was not limited to publishing a paper; it provided a deployable mixed-traffic control framework and research direction that later researchers adopted and extended to evaluate how learning-based CAV controllers should be trained, generalized, and made more reliable for real-world traffic deployment.
