Li et al. (2021) did not merely cite my publication as background literature. Their work adopted and extended the core contribution of my mixed-traffic research: that human-driven vehicles following autonomous vehicles should be treated as a distinct behavioral condition, rather than being assumed equivalent to ordinary human-following-human traffic. My field-experiment work provided one of the early empirical foundations for this distinction by showing that human drivers’ following behavior can change when the lead vehicle is recognizable as an autonomous vehicle, and that such changes depend on the driver’s subjective trust in AV technology.

Building on that contribution, Li et al. (2021) used the HV-following-AV distinction as a basis for evaluating operational safety in mixed traffic. They separately analyzed human-following-human, AV-following-human, and human-following-AV car-following conditions, calibrated car-following behavior models, and further examined behavior prediction for AVs and surrounding vehicles. In this way, their study extended my controlled field-experiment contribution into a broader real-world operational safety framework using AV operational data. This is significant because it shows that my work supplied a substantive behavioral framework that later researchers used to structure safety evaluation and prediction in mixed AV-human traffic, rather than serving only as a cited publication.
