Re: Verification of Dr. Yu Wang’s Research Contributions
Dear Officer:
I am providing this letter to verify Dr. Yu Wang’s substantive contributions to several collaborative research publications on proactive longitudinal control of connected and autonomous vehicles in mixed-flow traffic. Across these studies, Dr. Wang’s contributions spanned multiple important dimensions of the research, including model formulation, data-processing logic, experimental design, result interpretation, and manuscript development. Dr. Wang provided concrete technical input that helped shape how the research problems were formulated, how the learning-based control frameworks were designed, and how the numerical experiments were structured to demonstrate the effectiveness and practical relevance of the proposed methods.
In particular, Dr. Wang made substantive contributions to the following publications:
1. “Proactive Longitudinal Control to Manage Disruptive Lane Changes of Human-Driven Vehicles in Mixed-Flow Traffic,” IFAC PapersOnLine, 2021.
Reward-function and control-design input: Dr. Wang contributed to the design logic of the reward-function structure, particularly the need to balance lane-change preclusion with safety distance, motion comfort, and speed stability. Her input helped ensure that the controller was not optimized only to prevent lane changes, but was also constrained to avoid unsafe or overly aggressive longitudinal behavior by the CAV.
Data-processing and scenario-construction input: Dr. Wang provided input on how the empirical trajectory data should be used to construct a meaningful mixed-traffic case study. In particular, she advised on using congested NGSIM trajectories as the behavioral basis for the surrounding vehicles and structuring the scenario so that the speed difference between vehicles in adjacent lanes could create realistic conditions for a potential disruptive lane change. This helped connect the simulation setup to observable human-driving patterns rather than relying solely on artificial vehicle trajectories.
Impact on the paper: These contributions strengthened the technical credibility of the case study. By combining empirical trajectory-based vehicle motions with IDM/MOBIL-based human-driver behavior and a reward function that balanced preclusion, safety, comfort, and speed stability, the paper was able to show more specifically that the proposed CAV controller could reduce disruptive lane-change conditions without simply producing an aggressive blocking behavior.
2. “Proactive Longitudinal Control of Connected and Autonomous Vehicles with Lane-Change Assistance for Human-Driven Vehicles,” IEEE ITSC, 2021.
Modeling contribution: Dr. Wang provided input on the behavior-prediction component, including the need to predict not only the target vehicle’s longitudinal state and lane-change intention, but also its lane-change trajectory before switching to the lane-change assistance controller. This helped make the assistance strategy responsive to the anticipated interaction between the CAV and the human-driven vehicle, rather than treating the lane change as a fixed disturbance.
Evaluation input: Dr. Wang advised on how to evaluate the proposed assistance strategy against the baseline EIDM controller, including the use of acceleration variance and acceleration fluctuation to measure whether the controller reduced abrupt braking and improved smoothness during the lane-change process. She also suggested presenting the comparison across different HDV driver types so that the results would not appear limited to one selected human-driving profile.
Impact on the paper: Her contributions helped clarify that the proposed method was not simply a lane-change prediction model, but a proactive control strategy that used prediction to support smoother and safer CAV–HDV interaction. The added evaluation structure made the results more persuasive by showing that the controller improved smoothness during lane-change assistance across aggressive, normal, and cautious driver settings.
3. “Proactive Longitudinal Control to Preclude Disruptive Lane Changes of Human-Driven Vehicles in Mixed-Flow Traffic,” Control Engineering Practice, 2023.
Research-framing contribution: Dr. Wang helped refine the technical framing of the journal article by clarifying that the purpose of the proactive longitudinal control strategy was not to directly block a human-driven vehicle after it had initiated a lane change. Rather, the strategy should be framed as proactively reducing traffic conditions that are likely to induce a disruptive lane change before the maneuver begins, while allowing the system to switch back to car-following control if the human-driven vehicle proceeds with the lane change. This distinction helped position the method as a safe and interaction-aware control framework rather than an unsafe blocking strategy.
Robustness-design input: Dr. Wang advised on broadening the numerical experiments beyond a single selected case. Her input helped shape the inclusion of different lane-change scenarios, CAV control setups, and HDV driver types, so that the paper could demonstrate the generalizability of the proposed framework under a wider range of mixed-traffic interactions.
Impact on the paper: These contributions helped transform the work from an initial proactive-control concept into a more rigorous and generalizable journal article. The refined framing addressed an important safety concern by distinguishing proactive traffic-condition management from direct lane-change blocking, while the expanded robustness analysis strengthened the evidence that the framework could improve string stability and traffic smoothness across different mixed-traffic conditions.
Across these publications, Dr. Wang’s role was not limited to general discussion or editorial review. She provided concrete technical input that helped shape the formulation of the CAV–HDV interaction problems, improve the control-design logic, refine the data and experiment structures, and strengthen the interpretation of the results. Based on my direct collaboration with Dr. Wang, I consider her contributions to these publications to be substantive and important.
