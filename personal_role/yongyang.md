Re: Verification of Dr. Yu Wang’s Research Contributions
Dear Officer:
I am providing this letter to confirm and describe Dr. Yu Wang’s substantive contributions to several collaborative research publications on proactive longitudinal control of connected and autonomous vehicles (CAVs) in mixed-flow traffic, where CAVs coexist with human-driven vehicles (HDVs). Across these studies, Dr. Wang’s contributions spanned multiple critical aspects of the research, including model formulation, data-processing logic, experimental design, result interpretation, and manuscript development. Dr. Wang provided concrete technical contributions that influenced the formulation of the research questions, the development of the control and learning frameworks, and the design of the validation experiments used to demonstrate the effectiveness, robustness, and real-world relevance of the resulting research outcomes.
Dr. Wang made substantive contributions to the following publications:
1. “Proactive Longitudinal Control to Preclude Disruptive Lane Changes of Human-Driven Vehicles in Mixed-Flow Traffic,” Control Engineering Practice, 2023.
●Research-framing contribution: Dr. Wang provided important intellectual input on how the proactive longitudinal control strategy should be formulated and interpreted within the context of mixed-traffic interactions. She emphasized that the objective of the CAV controller should not be to directly block an HDV after a lane-change maneuver had already been initiated. Instead, the controller should proactively adjust the surrounding traffic conditions to reduce the likelihood of disruptive lane changes before they occur, while retaining the ability to transition back to conventional car-following control if a lane change ultimately proceeds. This distinction helped establish the framework as a human-aware and safety-conscious control strategy that manages lane-change incentives rather than directly interfering with driver behavior in an unsafe manner.  
●Robustness-design input: Dr. Wang contributed to the design of the numerical evaluation framework by emphasizing the need to assess controller performance under a diverse set of mixed-traffic conditions rather than relying on a single representative scenario. Her input led to the inclusion of different lane-change scenarios, CAV control configurations, and HDV driver profiles. These additions enabled a more comprehensive assessment of the controller’s robustness and applicability across heterogeneous traffic environments. 
●Impacts on the paper: Dr. Wang’s contributions strengthened both the conceptual foundation and the technical rigor of the study. Her guidance helped clarify the role of proactive longitudinal control as a mechanism for managing disruptive lane-change conditions while preserving safe interactions between CAVs and human drivers. In addition, the expanded evaluation framework provided stronger evidence that the proposed control strategy could improve traffic smoothness and string stability across a broad range of mixed-traffic scenarios, thereby enhancing the credibility and generalizability of the research findings.
2. “Proactive Longitudinal Control to Assist Lane Changes of Human-Driven Vehicles in Mixed Traffic: Human-Emulation Approach,” Automotive Innovation, 2025.
●Modeling contribution: Dr. Wang provided technical input on the integration of the behavior-prediction and lane-change assistance components within the proposed control framework. In particular, she emphasized that the controller should account not only for the likelihood of a lane change, but also for how the anticipated maneuver could influence subsequent interactions between CAVs and HDVs. Her input helped refine the design logic of the prediction-informed assistance strategy, enabling the controller to respond proactively to anticipated lane-change behavior rather than treating the maneuver as a fixed disturbance.
●Evaluation input: Dr. Wang contributed to the design of the performance evaluation methodology used to assess the proposed lane-change assistance strategy. She recommended evaluating the controller not only with respect to safety, but also in terms of traffic smoothness and vehicle motion stability, including measures that capture acceleration variability and fluctuations associated with abrupt braking behavior. In addition, she emphasized the importance of examining controller performance across different HDV driver profiles so that the findings would reflect a broader range of human-driving behaviors in the real world.
●Impacts on the paper: Dr. Wang’s contributions helped strengthen both the technical rationale and the validation framework of the study. Her input clarified that the proposed approach was not merely a lane-change prediction model, but a proactive control strategy that leveraged prediction to facilitate smoother and safer interactions between CAVs and HDVs. Furthermore, the expanded evaluation framework provided stronger evidence of the controller’s effectiveness across heterogeneous human driver populations, thereby enhancing the robustness, practical relevance, and generalizability of the research findings.
3. “Navigating in Mixed Traffic: A Human-Centric Approach to Manage Lane Changes of Human-Driven Vehicles,” IEEE T-ITS, 2026.
●Reward-function and control-design input: Dr. Wang contributed to the design logic of the reward-function structure used in the proposed human-centric control framework. In particular, she emphasized the importance of balancing lane-change management objectives with other operational considerations, including safety distance maintenance, motion comfort, and speed stability. Her input helped ensure that the controller was not optimized only to manage lane changes but also incorporated safeguards against unsafe or overly aggressive longitudinal control actions by CAVs.
●Data-processing and scenario-construction input: Dr. Wang contributed to the development of the mixed-traffic case study by providing input on the use of empirical trajectory data and the construction of representative traffic scenarios. She advised on leveraging congested NGSIM vehicle trajectories to characterize the behavior of surrounding vehicles and on structuring the traffic environment so that speed differences between adjacent lanes could naturally create opportunities for disruptive lane changes. These recommendations helped ground the simulation framework in observable human-driving behavior rather than relying exclusively on artificially generated traffic conditions. 
●Impacts on the paper: Dr. Wang’s contributions strengthened the realism and technical rigor of the study. Her input helped establish a more balanced evaluation framework in which lane-change management objectives were considered alongside safety, comfort, and traffic-stability considerations. In addition, the use of empirically grounded traffic scenarios improved the credibility of the case study and provided stronger evidence that the proposed controller could reduce disruptive lane-change conditions while maintaining safe and reasonable interactions between CAVs and HDVs.
Across these publications, Dr. Wang’s role extended well beyond general discussion or editorial review. She provided concrete technical input that strengthened the conceptual formulation, methodological rigor, and experimental evaluation of the research. Based on my direct collaboration with Dr. Wang throughout these projects, I consider her contributions to these publications to be substantive, technically significant, and important to the overall quality of the research.
