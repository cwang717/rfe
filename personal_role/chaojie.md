Re: Verification of Dr. Yu Wang’s Contributions to Cooperative Roundabout Control Strategy for Connected and Autonomous Vehicles
Dear Officer:
I am writing to verify Dr. Yu Wang’s contributions to our co-authored article, Cooperative Roundabout Control Strategy for Connected and Autonomous Vehicles, published in Applied Sciences in 2022. I served as the first author of this paper, and Dr. Wang contributed to the study as a postdoctoral mentor and research collaborator.
In developing this work, Dr. Wang provided substantive guidance on the research design and technical direction. One of her key contributions was helping us define the study around a more specific and meaningful question: whether connected and autonomous vehicles could make the circular-flow structure of roundabouts more effective than conventional cross-flow intersection control. This guidance helped move the paper beyond a general CAV intersection-control problem and toward a structured framework that linked system-level traffic efficiency with vehicle-level safety and stability.
Dr. Wang also contributed to the development and verification of the simulation-based analysis. She helped review the approach-level demand settings, initial queue conditions, turning proportions, and segment-capacity assumptions used in the numerical study. Her input was important in ensuring that the simulation design properly reflected the logic of the proposed roundabout flow-control model.
In addition, Dr. Wang helped strengthen the case-study component of the paper by encouraging us to include experiments that directly tested the practical function of the proposed framework. For example, she advised us to examine whether the flow-control model could adapt merge-in decisions across the four approaches as queue lengths changed, and whether the virtual platoon-control component could stabilize vehicles after merge-in disturbances. These additions helped demonstrate that the proposed method was not only theoretically formulated, but also capable of supporting efficient and stable vehicle operations in a simulated CAV roundabout environment.
Overall, Dr. Wang’s role was meaningful and technically substantive. While I led the manuscript as first author, Dr. Wang’s mentorship and research input helped shape the problem formulation, improve the simulation validation, and clarify the broader significance of the results. Her contributions were therefore important to the quality and impact of the final publication.
Sincerely,
Chaojie Wang, Ph.D.
