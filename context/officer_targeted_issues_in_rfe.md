# Officer-Targeted Issues in the EB-1A RFE

**Case:** Yu Wang — Form I-140, EB-1A / E11 Extraordinary Ability  
**Field identified by USCIS:** Transportation Engineering  
**Intended role identified by USCIS:** Lead Autonomy Engineer  
**RFE focus:** USCIS accepted two regulatory criteria but challenged the claimed **original contributions of major significance** and the broader **final merits** showing.

---

## Executive Summary

The officer does **not** appear to challenge the entire petition. The RFE accepts that the beneficiary meets:

1. **Judging the work of others**; and  
2. **Authorship of scholarly articles**.

The officer’s case-specific concern is that the petition has not yet established a third criterion: **original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of major significance in the field**.

The RFE’s central message is:

> The submitted evidence may show publications, citations, support letters, and work in an important field, but USCIS wants objective, independent documentary evidence proving that the beneficiary’s **specific personal contributions** were both **original** and **of major significance to the field**.

---

## Accepted Criteria

### 1. Judging Criterion — Accepted

USCIS states that sufficient evidence was submitted to show that the beneficiary participated, individually or on a panel, as a judge of the work of others in the same or allied field.

**Status:** Met.

### 2. Authorship Criterion — Accepted

USCIS states that sufficient evidence was submitted to show authorship of scholarly articles in the field, in professional or major trade publications or other major media.

**Status:** Met.

---

## Main Officer-Targeted Issue: Original Contributions of Major Significance

### Issue 1: Support Letters Are Not Enough Without Independent Documentary Evidence

The officer repeatedly emphasizes that letters of support alone do not establish the criterion.

USCIS says the petitioner made claims regarding original contributions, but did not submit enough independent documentary evidence to support those claims. The officer specifically notes that USCIS may consider expert letters, but is not required to treat them as conclusive, especially if the opinions are not corroborated by objective evidence.

**Practical meaning:**  
The response should not rely mainly on recommendation letters. Letters can explain the importance of the work, but the core proof should be external records, public documents, third-party publications, implementation records, technical reports, contracts, standards, agency documents, product documentation, or other objective evidence.

**Response priority:** High.

---

### Issue 2: The Officer Questions Whether the Work Was Truly “Original”

The RFE states that the petitioner did not submit enough evidence showing that the work was original and not merely built upon established research by others.

This is not just a generic objection. The officer specifically focuses on whether the beneficiary’s work added something new, rather than being another research paper within an existing research stream.

**Practical meaning:**  
The response should clearly define each claimed contribution and explain:

- What problem existed before the beneficiary’s work;
- What prior methods failed to solve;
- What the beneficiary introduced that was new;
- How the contribution differed from prior work;
- Why the contribution was not merely incremental.

**Response priority:** High.

---

### Issue 3: The Officer Questions Personal Attribution

The officer notes that there were other researchers who contributed to the research and that the petitioner has not shown that the beneficiary should be credited with the overall contribution of major significance, if any.

This is one of the most important targeted issues.

**Practical meaning:**  
For coauthored papers, team projects, government reports, company work, or collaborative studies, the response must isolate the beneficiary’s own role.

The response should show:

- Which specific idea, model, method, dataset, experiment, framework, or analytical tool was created by the beneficiary;
- Whether the beneficiary was first author, corresponding author, lead contributor, principal designer, or primary analyst;
- Statements from collaborators confirming the beneficiary’s specific contribution;
- Evidence from papers, acknowledgments, contribution statements, project records, code repositories, or documentation showing individual contribution;
- Why the field-level impact should be attributed to the beneficiary rather than to the team generally.

**Response priority:** Very high.

---

### Issue 4: Citations Alone Do Not Prove Major Significance

The officer states that there are many research articles in reputable journals that are regularly cited, but not every cited article has made a significant contribution to the field.

This means USCIS is not persuaded by citation count alone.

**Practical meaning:**  
The response should not simply present more citation numbers. Instead, it should explain the substance of the citations.

Strong citation evidence should show that later researchers:

- Adopted the beneficiary’s method;
- Used the beneficiary’s data;
- Built systems based on the beneficiary’s framework;
- Used the work as a benchmark;
- Extended the work into new research directions;
- Relied on the work in government, industry, or regulatory contexts;
- Treated the work as foundational, rare, first-of-its-kind, or field-changing.

**Response priority:** High.

---

### Issue 5: The Officer Wants Proof of Adoption, Implementation, or Use by Others

The RFE specifically lists possible evidence of major significance, including:

- Evidence that the work is being implemented by others;
- Contracts with companies using the beneficiary’s products;
- Licensed technology being used by others;
- Patents currently being utilized and shown to be significant;
- Evidence that the work provoked widespread public commentary;
- Evidence that people throughout the field currently consider the work important.

**Practical meaning:**  
This is a roadmap for the response. The strongest response should organize the evidence around actual adoption and implementation.

Useful evidence may include:

- Government technical reports using the beneficiary’s model, data, framework, or method;
- Company research papers or technical documents applying the beneficiary’s approach;
- Public project documentation;
- Agency publications;
- Product or platform documentation;
- Standards or guideline documents;
- Grant reports;
- Procurement or contract records;
- Citations that explicitly describe the beneficiary’s work as a basis, benchmark, protocol, dataset, or method;
- Independent third-party explanations connecting the beneficiary’s work to real-world use.

**Response priority:** Very high.

---

### Issue 6: Government Agency Claims Need Objective Proof

The officer specifically identifies claims that the work was adopted or used by federal and state agencies, including:

- California;
- Florida;
- Texas;
- Virginia;
- U.S. Department of Transportation;
- European Commission.

The officer says the petition submitted only support letters making these claims, without enough independent documentary evidence.

**Practical meaning:**  
This is a highly targeted criticism. The response should directly address each agency or jurisdiction named by the officer.

For each claimed agency use, the response should provide:

1. The agency or public entity;
2. The document, report, project, program, or study showing use;
3. The exact portion where the beneficiary’s work is cited or applied;
4. A short explanation of what was used;
5. A short explanation of why that use shows major significance;
6. If possible, independent corroboration beyond a recommendation letter.

**Response priority:** Very high.

---

### Issue 7: Importance of the Field Is Not Enough

The officer states that merely showing government initiatives in the field is insufficient. Working in an important field, showing that the field has government initiatives, or showing that the endeavor is STEM-related does not prove that the beneficiary’s contribution was original or of major significance.

**Practical meaning:**  
Arguments about autonomous driving, transportation safety, traffic efficiency, STEM importance, or national interest should be secondary. They do not replace evidence that the beneficiary personally made a major original contribution.

The response should avoid over-relying on statements like:

- Autonomous driving is important;
- Transportation engineering is important;
- The U.S. government invests in this area;
- The work relates to safety, energy, emissions, or infrastructure;
- The field benefits the United States.

Those points can provide context, but the key question is still:

> What did the beneficiary personally contribute, and how did the field use it?

**Response priority:** Medium to high.

---

### Issue 8: The Officer Sees Possible Overlap Between Authorship and Original Contribution

The officer states that the claimed original contributions appear to be research articles that are also being claimed under the authorship criterion. USCIS emphasizes that the EB-1A criteria are separate and distinct and cannot be treated as interchangeable.

**Practical meaning:**  
The response should not argue that “because the papers were published, the original contribution criterion is met.”

Instead, the response should distinguish:

- **Authorship evidence:** The beneficiary wrote scholarly articles.
- **Original contribution evidence:** The underlying methods, data, theories, experiments, models, or tools from those articles changed the field, were adopted by others, or became important references for later work.

**Response priority:** High.

---

### Issue 9: Final Merits Will Still Be Reviewed Even If Three Criteria Are Met

The officer says that even if the petitioner establishes at least three criteria, USCIS will then evaluate all evidence in a final merits determination.

The final merits review asks whether the beneficiary has:

- Sustained national or international acclaim; and
- Achievements recognized in the field showing that the beneficiary is among the small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field.

**Practical meaning:**  
The RFE response should not only prove the third criterion. It should also explain why the total record shows top-level recognition and sustained acclaim.

The response should connect:

- Accepted judging evidence;
- Accepted authorship evidence;
- Strengthened original contribution evidence;
- Independent use by agencies, companies, and researchers;
- Evidence of ongoing influence;
- Evidence of recognition by independent experts;
- Evidence that the work has practical and theoretical impact beyond ordinary academic publication.

**Response priority:** High.

---

## Recommended Response Structure

### Section 1: Brief Overview of What USCIS Accepted

State that USCIS has already found the judging and authorship criteria met.

### Section 2: Directly Address Original Contributions

Define each claimed contribution separately. For each contribution, use a consistent structure:

1. **Contribution name**
2. **What was original**
3. **Beneficiary’s personal role**
4. **Objective evidence of adoption or reliance**
5. **Why the adoption/reliance shows major significance**
6. **Supporting expert letter, used only as explanation**

### Section 3: Agency-by-Agency Rebuttal

Create a table addressing the officer’s named agencies:

| Agency / Entity | Officer Concern | Objective Evidence to Submit | How It Shows Major Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| California | Claimed use not independently proven | Public report / citation / project document | Shows practical use in transportation policy or implementation |
| Florida | Claimed use not independently proven | AMS tool report / research project document | Shows use of data or method in modeling/simulation |
| Texas | Claimed policy/regulatory impact not independently proven | TxDOT document / public project / technical report | Shows relevance to state transportation practice |
| Virginia | Claimed adoption not independently proven | VTRC report / public citation | Shows use in safety or automation framework |
| U.S. DOT / FHWA | Claimed federal impact not independently proven | FHWA technical report | Shows federal-level reliance |
| European Commission | Claimed international regulatory impact not independently proven | EC/JRC report or project document | Shows international governmental reliance |

### Section 4: Distinguish Publications from Contributions

Explain that the publications are the vehicle through which the contributions were disseminated, but the claimed contributions are the underlying methods, models, datasets, empirical findings, frameworks, or tools that others adopted.

### Section 5: Final Merits Argument

Conclude by showing that the accepted criteria plus the strengthened contribution evidence demonstrate sustained acclaim and recognition at the top of the field.

---

## Evidence Most Likely to Matter

The most useful new or reorganized evidence would be:

1. Public government reports citing or using the beneficiary’s work;
2. Screenshots or excerpts from agency reports showing specific reliance;
3. Third-party technical papers that implemented the beneficiary’s method or data;
4. Citation-context analysis showing substantive use rather than background citation;
5. Independent documents from companies or agencies showing adoption;
6. Coauthor or collaborator statements explaining the beneficiary’s personal contribution;
7. Evidence that the beneficiary’s model, dataset, method, or framework became a benchmark or protocol;
8. Evidence that later work achieved concrete results by using the beneficiary’s contribution;
9. Tables mapping each officer objection to specific exhibits;
10. A clear explanation separating field importance from personal contribution.

---

## One-Sentence Core Strategy

The RFE response should prove, with objective third-party documents, that the beneficiary’s specific original work was adopted, implemented, relied upon, or used as a benchmark by agencies, companies, and researchers, and that this impact is personally attributable to the beneficiary rather than merely to publications, coauthors, or the general importance of autonomous driving.
