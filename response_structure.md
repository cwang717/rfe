# Writing Rules for the Agent

## 1. Use plain-English titles

Prefer:
"Dr. Wang produced real-world evidence showing how human drivers behave around autonomous vehicles."

Avoid:
"Controlled mixed-traffic empirical dataset and AV appearance behavioral adaptation framework."

## 2. Define technical terms on first use

Examples:

* Mixed traffic: roads where autonomous vehicles and human-driven vehicles operate together.
* Car-following model: a model that predicts how one vehicle follows another.
* Stochastic: accounting for uncertainty and variation.
* Trajectory optimization: choosing vehicle speed and path to improve safety, travel time, fuel use, or comfort.
* Benchmark: a reference method or dataset used by others to test their own systems.

## 3. Use evidence verbs

Use:

* used;
* adopted;
* extended;
* benchmarked;
* calibrated;
* validated;
* relied upon;
* incorporated into;
* served as a foundation for;
* sought her expertise;
* used her industry-grounded insight;
* invited her to train government-side professionals.

Avoid unsupported:

* revolutionized;
* transformed the entire field;
* became a standard;
* was adopted by all major companies;
* changed regulation;
* commercialized by companies;
  unless directly documented.

## 4. Do not overstate agency or company implementation

If the evidence is a paper or report by researchers affiliated with a company, write:
"Company-affiliated researchers used or extended Dr. Wang's work."

Do not write:
"The company implemented Dr. Wang's method in its commercial product"
unless there is product documentation, contract evidence, license evidence, or a company letter specifically confirming implementation.

## 5. Letters must support, not substitute

Each expert, organizer, or PI letter should be paired with objective evidence:

* public report;
* citation-context excerpt;
* patent record;
* dataset documentation;
* paper excerpt;
* agenda;
* speaker list;
* slides;
* project documentation;
* training materials.

## 6. End every contribution with a clear mini-conclusion

Suggested sentence:
"This evidence shows major significance because independent users did not merely cite Dr. Wang's work as background; they used it to [specific use], demonstrating substantive reliance and practical translation in the field."
