Zhuo et al. (2023) provides objective third-party evidence that my roundabout-specific planning and control contribution was used by later researchers as more than a background citation. My cooperative roundabout control work introduced a structure-specific framework for CAV operation at roundabouts: it treated the roundabout not as a generic intersection, but as a circular-flow environment requiring coordinated merge-in decisions, platoon-based vehicle sequencing, and stable vehicle-level control. Zhuo et al. adopted this same technical framing—CAV platooning and cooperative operation in a roundabout environment—and extended it to a new mixed-traffic deployment question: how different CAV platoon configurations affect roundabout performance when CAVs and human-driven vehicles coexist. Specifically, instead of merely discussing my article, they built their study around the operational concepts my work helped establish for roundabouts, including coordinated CAV platoons, throughput and delay as system-level performance measures, and roundabout-specific interaction constraints. They then expanded the framework by evaluating maximum platoon size, platoon willingness, and homogeneous versus heterogeneous platoon types under different CAV penetration rates, traffic flows, and turning percentages. This shows that my contribution supplied a practical technical foundation for later researchers to investigate deployable CAV control in roundabouts, moving the field beyond generic intersection control toward structure-specific mixed-traffic planning and control methods.
